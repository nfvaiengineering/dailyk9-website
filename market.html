Markers

4 words that help you teach your dog anything

A marker takes a picture of the exact moment your dog got it right. Mark within 3 seconds. Treat a few seconds after, so it lands on what you marked. Same words, every person in the house.

Got it. Go get the treat I tossed. The one that teaches Place fast.

Done. Released. No treat when you're building duration. The release is yours.

Reward markers - a treat follows. Release marker - no treat.

Try it tonight - 5 minutes

  1. Catch the look. Your dog looks at you on their own: "Yes" and hand them a treat. 10 reps.
  2. Chase game. Toss a treat: "Get it." When they turn back to you: "Yes" and a treat from your hand.
  3. Hold it. Dog sitting in front of you: "Good," treat, "Good," treat. Then "Break."
The foundation cue

Place in 60 seconds

"Place" means 4 feet on that thing and settle into a down. Any bed, mat, or raised cot. Everything else you teach builds on it - it's how you get a dog who can switch off at a restaurant.

Words from the markers above: "Good" = hold it, the treat comes to you. "Get it" = go get the tossed treat. "Break" = done, no treat. Mark within 3 seconds, treat a few seconds after.

How to teach it

  1. Load the bed. Toss a few treats on the bed and let them find them.
  2. 4 feet on. When all 4 feet land: "Good" and a treat on the bed.
  3. Reset with "Get it". Toss a treat past the bed: "Get it." They come back, 4 feet on: "Good." 10 reps. Then start saying "Place" as they step on.
  4. Build the settle. Treat more when they lie down. Wait a little longer between treats each round.
  5. Release. End with "Break" - no treat after. Treat after the release and they learn getting up pays.
  6. Add life. 2 steps away, then the doorbell, then dinner. If they get up, go back a step.
